## 📖 Part 1: Understanding Coaching vs. Mentoring vs. Managing (15 minutes)

### The Three Roles:

```
┌─────────────┬──────────────┬─────────────┬──────────────┐
│             │  MANAGING    │  COACHING   │  MENTORING   │
├─────────────┼──────────────┼─────────────┼──────────────┤
│ FOCUS       │ Performance  │ Development │ Career &     │
│             │ & Results    │ & Growth    │ Wisdom       │
├─────────────┼──────────────┼─────────────┼──────────────┤
│ TIME FRAME  │ Immediate    │ Medium-term │ Long-term    │
│             │ (this quarter)│ (this year) │ (career)     │
├─────────────┼──────────────┼─────────────┼──────────────┤
│ APPROACH    │ Directive    │ Facilitative│ Advisory     │
│             │ "Do this"    │ "What could │ "Here's what │
│             │              │ you try?"   │ I learned"   │
├─────────────┼──────────────┼─────────────┼──────────────┤
│ EXPERTISE   │ Manager has  │ Coachee has │ Mentor has   │
│ SOURCE      │ answers      │ answers     │ experience   │
├─────────────┼──────────────┼─────────────┼──────────────┤
│ STRUCTURE   │ Required     │ Structured  │ Flexible     │
│             │ (formal)     │ sessions    │ relationship │
├─────────────┼──────────────┼─────────────┼──────────────┤
│ BEST FOR    │ Delivering   │ Building    │ Navigating   │
│             │ objectives   │ skills      │ career path  │
└─────────────┴──────────────┴─────────────┴──────────────┘
```

### Examples of Each:

**Managing Conversation:**
"Sarah, the Q3 report is due Friday. I need you to finish the analysis by Wednesday so we have time to review. Let's meet tomorrow at 10 AM to check your progress."

**Coaching Conversation:**
"Sarah, I notice your presentations could have more impact. What's one aspect you'd like to improve? What's stopping you from doing that now? What could you try differently in next week's presentation?"

**Mentoring Conversation:**
"Sarah, when I was at your career stage, I struggled with the same tension between deep expertise and broad management. Here's what I learned about making that transition..."

### When to Use Each:

**Managing:**
- Urgent deadlines or crises
- Clear performance gaps
- Directing specific tasks
- Setting expectations

**Coaching:**
- Developing capabilities
- Solving recurring challenges
- Building problem-solving skills
- Preparing for next level

**Mentoring:**
- Career decision-making
- Navigating organizational politics
- Industry knowledge transfer
- Long-term development

**You need all three, but know which hat you're wearing.**

## 📖 Part 2: The GROW Coaching Model (25 minutes)

### GROW Framework:

The GROW model is the most widely-used coaching framework in business.

```
G - GOAL       What do you want to achieve?
R - REALITY    What's happening now?
O - OPTIONS    What could you do?
W - WAY FORWARD What will you do?
```

### Detailed Breakdown:

#### **G - GOAL (What do you want?)**

**Purpose:** Establish clear, specific objectives

**Questions to Ask:**
- "What would you like to focus on today?"
- "What would make this conversation valuable for you?"
- "What would success look like?"
- "How will you know you've achieved this?"
- "What's the bigger picture this fits into?"
- "What's most important about this goal?"

**Example:**
```
Coach: "What would you like to focus on today?"

Coachee: "I want to get better at presenting to executives."

Coach: "That's great. What specifically about executive presentations would
you like to improve?"

Coachee: "I get really nervous and rush through my slides."

Coach: "So if you could manage your nerves and pace yourself effectively,
what would that enable you to do?"

Coachee: "I'd be able to have more impact and influence strategic decisions."

Coach: "Perfect. So our goal is to help you present with confidence and
appropriate pacing so you can influence executive decisions. How does that
sound as our focus?"
```

**Key Principle:** Make goals SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)

#### **R - REALITY (What's happening now?)**

**Purpose:** Understand current situation objectively

**Questions to Ask:**
- "What's happening now?"
- "When does this occur?"
- "How often does this happen?"
- "What have you tried so far?"
- "What's working? What isn't?"
- "Who else is involved?"
- "What's the impact on you and others?"

**Example:**
```
Coach: "Tell me more about what happens when you present to executives."

Coachee: "I prepared for 10 hours for the last one, but when I walked in
the room, my heart was racing. I rushed through 20 slides in 15 minutes
and barely looked up from my laptop."

Coach: "What happens specifically when you start feeling nervous?"

Coachee: "My hands shake a bit, I talk faster, and I just want to get
through it as quickly as possible."

Coach: "How do the executives typically respond?"

Coachee: "They look confused or ask me to slow down. Sometimes they cut me
off with questions I'm not prepared for, which makes me more nervous."

Coach: "What have you tried to manage this?"

Coachee: "I practice a lot and try to memorize my script, but that doesn't
seem to help in the moment."
```

**Key Principle:** Reality is about observation, not judgment. Stay curious.

#### **O - OPTIONS (What could you do?)**

**Purpose:** Brainstorm possible solutions

**Questions to Ask:**
- "What could you do to address this?"
- "What else?"
- "If you had no constraints, what would you try?"
- "What would you advise a friend to do?"
- "What's worked for you in similar situations?"
- "Who do you know who handles this well? What do they do?"
- "What haven't you tried yet?"

**Example:**
```
Coach: "What are some things you could try to manage your nerves and pacing?"

Coachee: "I could practice more... but I already practice a lot."

Coach: "What else might help?"

Coachee: "Maybe I could practice presenting to a colleague first?"

Coach: "Good. What else?"

Coachee: "I could use breathing techniques before I present."

Coach: "Yes. What other options can you think of?"

Coachee: "I could reduce my slide count and plan for more discussion."

Coach: "Interesting. If you had no constraints at all, what would make you
feel most confident?"

Coachee: "Actually getting feedback from an executive on my approach before
the real presentation. And maybe not using slides at all—just telling the
story."

Coach: "Now we're getting somewhere. What else?"

[Continue until you have 5-7 options]
```

**Key Principle:** Generate multiple options before evaluating any. Quantity before quality.

#### **W - WAY FORWARD (What will you do?)**

**Purpose:** Commit to specific actions

**Questions to Ask:**
- "Which option feels most promising?"
- "What will you do first?"
- "When will you do this?"
- "What support do you need?"
- "How will you know it's working?"
- "What might get in your way?"
- "On a scale of 1-10, how committed are you?"
- "What would make it a 10?"

**Example:**
```
Coach: "Looking at all these options, what feels most promising to you?"

Coachee: "I think reducing slides and focusing on storytelling, plus getting
feedback from my VP beforehand."

Coach: "Great. What's your first step?"

Coachee: "I'll schedule 30 minutes with my VP this week to walk through my
approach and get feedback."

Coach: "When specifically will you do that?"

Coachee: "I'll send the meeting invite today for Wednesday afternoon."

Coach: "Perfect. And the storytelling approach—what will you do to develop
that?"

Coachee: "I'll outline my next presentation as a narrative with just 5 key
slides instead of 20."

Coach: "When?"

Coachee: "This Friday."

Coach: "How committed are you to doing these things, on a scale of 1-10?"

Coachee: "Probably an 8."

Coach: "What would make it a 10?"

Coachee: "If I had someone to practice my narrative approach with before
the real presentation."

Coach: "Can you arrange that?"

Coachee: "Yes, I'll ask David from my team to be my practice audience."

Coach: "Excellent. So to recap, you'll: 1) Schedule feedback session with
VP by end of day, 2) Outline narrative approach by Friday, and 3) Practice
with David before your next executive presentation. How does that sound?"

Coachee: "Perfect. I feel much more confident now."
```

**Key Principle:** Get specific commitments with timelines. Vague intentions don't drive change.

## 📖 Part 3: The CLEAR Coaching Framework (15 minutes)

### CLEAR Model:

An alternative framework that emphasizes relationship and review:

```
C - CONTRACTING    Establish agreement for coaching
L - LISTENING      Understand deeply through active listening
E - EXPLORING      Examine situation and possibilities
A - ACTION         Commit to specific steps
R - REVIEW         Assess progress and learning
```

### When to Use CLEAR vs. GROW:

**GROW:** Best for single sessions, specific challenges
**CLEAR:** Better for ongoing coaching relationships

### CLEAR in Practice:

#### **C - CONTRACTING**

Establish the coaching relationship and expectations:

**Topics to Cover:**
- Purpose and goals of coaching
- Frequency and duration of sessions
- Confidentiality boundaries
- Roles and responsibilities
- How you'll measure success

**Example Contracting Conversation:**
```
"I'm excited to work with you on your leadership development. Let's make
sure we're aligned on how this will work.

I see my role as helping you discover your own solutions through questions
and reflection. I'm not here to give you answers or tell you what to do—
you're the expert on your situation.

I suggest we meet for 45 minutes every two weeks for the next six months.
Our conversations will be confidential—I won't share what we discuss with
anyone unless you explicitly ask me to or there's a serious concern about
safety or ethics.

Your role is to come prepared with topics you want to explore, be honest
about what's working and what isn't, and follow through on commitments you
make during our sessions.

Does this sound good to you? What questions or concerns do you have about
how we'll work together?"
```

#### **L - LISTENING**

Deep, active listening is the foundation:

**Active Listening Techniques:**
- Give full attention (no phone, laptop)
- Maintain appropriate eye contact
- Notice body language and tone
- Pause before responding
- Reflect back what you hear
- Ask clarifying questions
- Avoid interrupting
- Resist urge to give advice

**Reflective Listening Examples:**
- "What I'm hearing is..."
- "It sounds like you're feeling..."
- "So the challenge is..."
- "Help me understand..."

#### **E - EXPLORING**

Deep dive into the situation:

**Exploration Questions:**
- "Tell me more about that."
- "What else is true?"
- "What's the impact of this situation?"
- "What patterns do you notice?"
- "What assumptions are you making?"
- "What would you like to be different?"

#### **A - ACTION**

Commit to concrete next steps:

**Action Planning:**
- Specific actions
- Clear timelines
- Identified obstacles
- Support needed
- Accountability mechanism

#### **R - REVIEW**

Assess what's working:

**Review Questions:**
- "What's been most helpful?"
- "What have you learned?"
- "What's changed since we started?"
- "What do you want to focus on next?"
- "How is our coaching relationship working?"

## 📖 Part 4: Giving Powerful Feedback (20 minutes)

### The SBI Model:

**S**ituation: When and where
**B**ehavior: What you observed (facts, not interpretation)
**I**mpact: The effect of the behavior

#### **Examples:**

**Poor Feedback:**
"You're not a team player." (Vague, judgmental, unhelpful)

**SBI Feedback:**
"In yesterday's team meeting [SITUATION], when Sarah shared her idea, you
immediately said 'that won't work' without asking questions [BEHAVIOR].
The impact was that Sarah withdrew from the rest of the conversation and
the team didn't fully explore what might have been a valuable approach
[IMPACT]."

**Poor Feedback:**
"Great job on the presentation!" (Too vague to be useful)

**SBI Feedback:**
"In this morning's client presentation [SITUATION], you structured the
content to address each of their stated concerns and used specific data
from their industry [BEHAVIOR]. The client nodded throughout and asked to
schedule a follow-up meeting, which shows they found it relevant and
valuable [IMPACT]."

### Radical Candor Framework:

From Kim Scott's "Radical Candor"—effective feedback balances two dimensions:

```
        CHALLENGE DIRECTLY
               ↑
               │
               │  RADICAL CANDOR
               │  (Care + Challenge)
               │
    ───────────┼───────────→ CARE PERSONALLY
               │
               │  RUINOUS EMPATHY
               │  (Care without Challenge)
               │
               ↓
```

**Four Quadrants:**

1. **Radical Candor** (Care Personally + Challenge Directly)
   - Honest feedback delivered with genuine concern
   - BEST: Direct and kind
   - Example: "I care about your success, and I need to tell you this presentation missed the mark. Here's specifically what didn't work..."

2. **Ruinous Empathy** (Care Personally, Don't Challenge)
   - Avoiding hard conversations to spare feelings
   - Problem: Doesn't help them grow
   - Example: "The presentation was fine!" (when it wasn't)

3. **Obnoxious Aggression** (Challenge Directly, Don't Care)
   - Harsh criticism without relationship
   - Problem: Damages trust and motivation
   - Example: "That presentation was terrible. Do better next time."

4. **Manipulative Insincerity** (Neither Care nor Challenge)
   - Fake or political feedback
   - WORST: Neither honest nor kind
   - Example: *Saying nothing to their face, complaining to others*

**Aim for Radical Candor: Be direct AND kind.**

### The Feedback Sandwich (Use Carefully):

```
🍞 Positive feedback
🥩 Constructive feedback
🍞 Positive feedback
```

**Pros:**
- Softer delivery
- Maintains relationship
- Ends on positive note

**Cons:**
- Can feel manipulative if overused
- Middle message may get lost
- People learn to brace for "but..."

**Better Approach:** Give positive feedback frequently (not just when you have criticism). Then constructive feedback stands on its own.

### Feedforward vs. Feedback:

**Feedback:** Past-focused critique
- "You didn't prepare enough for that meeting."

**Feedforward:** Future-focused suggestions
- "For next week's meeting, what if you prepared three specific questions to ask the client?"

**Research shows feedforward is:**
- More motivating
- Less defensive
- More actionable
- Forward-looking

### Real-Time Feedback:

Don't wait for formal reviews. Give feedback immediately:

**Positive Feedback:**
"Hey, I just wanted to say that how you handled that difficult client question was impressive. You stayed calm, asked clarifying questions, and gave a thoughtful answer. Nice work."

**Constructive Feedback:**
"Can I give you some quick feedback? In the meeting just now when you interrupted Mark, it shut down the conversation. I know you were excited about your idea, but try waiting until he finishes next time. Sound good?"

## 📖 Part 5: Building a Mentorship Practice (15 minutes)

### Finding Mentees:

**Formal Programs:**
- Company mentorship programs
- Industry associations
- University alumni networks
- Professional organizations

**Informal Relationships:**
- Junior colleagues who seek advice
- People you notice with potential
- Referrals from your network
- Online communities (LinkedIn, industry forums)

### The First Mentorship Meeting:

**Agenda:**
1. **Get to know each other** (15 min)
   - Background and experience
   - Current role and responsibilities
   - Interests and passions

2. **Discuss goals and expectations** (15 min)
   - What does mentee hope to gain?
   - What can mentor offer?
   - Frequency and format of meetings

3. **Establish working norms** (10 min)
   - How will you communicate?
   - How will you handle confidentiality?
   - How will you give each other feedback?

4. **Plan next steps** (5 min)
   - Schedule next meeting
   - Any homework or preparation
   - Topic for next discussion

### Mentorship Meeting Agenda Template:

```
MENTORSHIP SESSION

Date: __________
Mentee: __________

CHECK-IN (5-10 min)
• How are you doing generally?
• What's new since we last met?
• Any quick wins or challenges?

FOCUS TOPIC (30-40 min)
What do you want to discuss today?
_________________________________________________

[Use coaching questions, share relevant experiences, offer perspective]

GUIDANCE & ADVICE (10-15 min)
Based on the discussion:
• Insights I can share: _________________________
• Suggestions to consider: ______________________
• Resources that might help: ___________________
• People you might want to meet: _______________

ACTION ITEMS (5 min)
What will you do before our next meeting?
1. _________________________________
2. _________________________________
3. _________________________________

What support do you need from me?
_________________________________________________

NEXT MEETING
Date: __________
Topic: __________
```

### Mentorship vs. Sponsorship:

**Mentorship:** Giving advice and guidance
- "Here's what I learned when..."
- "Have you considered...?"
- "Let me share my perspective..."

**Sponsorship:** Actively advocating for advancement
- "I recommended you for that task force."
- "I told the VP you're ready for more responsibility."
- "I'm putting your name forward for that promotion."

**Both are valuable. Sponsorship is often more career-changing.**

### Paying It Forward:

**Mentorship is how you multiply your impact.**

Calculate your impact:
- You directly lead: 10 people
- Each becomes effective leader: 10 × 10 = 100 people
- Their people become leaders: 100 × 10 = 1,000 people

**Your leadership compounds through the people you develop.**
