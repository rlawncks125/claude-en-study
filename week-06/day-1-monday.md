## 1️⃣ Interview Strategy & Types (20분)

### A. Interview Types

```
1. Phone Screening (전화 스크리닝)
Duration: 15-30 minutes
Purpose: Basic qualification check
Focus: Background, availability, salary expectations

2. Video Interview (화상 면접)
Duration: 30-60 minutes
Platform: Zoom, Teams, Google Meet
Focus: Technical skills, cultural fit

3. In-Person Interview (대면 면접)
Duration: 1-2 hours
Types:
- One-on-one
- Panel interview
- Group interview

4. Case Interview (케이스 면접)
Duration: 45-90 minutes
Focus: Problem-solving, analytical thinking
Common in: Consulting, strategy roles

5. Final Interview (최종 면접)
Duration: 30-60 minutes
With: C-level executives
Focus: Culture fit, motivation, leadership potential
```

### B. Interview Structure

```
Opening (5 minutes):
- Greeting and rapport building
- "Tell me about yourself"

Body (40-50 minutes):
- Experience questions
- Behavioral questions
- Technical questions
- Situational questions

Closing (5-10 minutes):
- Your questions
- Next steps
- Thank you
```

## 2️⃣ STAR Method (20분)

### STAR Framework

```
S - Situation (상황)
└─ 배경 설명, 2-3 sentences

T - Task (과제)
└─ 해결해야 할 문제, 1-2 sentences

A - Action (행동)
└─ 구체적으로 한 일, 3-4 sentences

R - Result (결과)
└─ 성과와 학습, 2-3 sentences
```

### Example 1: Leadership

**Question**: "Tell me about a time when you led a difficult project."

**STAR Answer**:

```
Situation:
"In my previous role as Project Manager at XYZ Corp, we were tasked
with implementing a new CRM system across five departments within
three months. The project had failed twice before under different
leadership."

Task:
"My task was to not only implement the system successfully but also
restore the team's confidence and ensure adoption across all
departments."

Action:
"I took several key actions. First, I conducted individual meetings
with stakeholders from each department to understand their concerns
and past failures. Second, I restructured the project timeline into
smaller, achievable milestones. Third, I established weekly check-ins
and created a feedback loop. Fourth, I personally trained super-users
in each department who could support their colleagues. Finally, I
celebrated small wins to build momentum."

Result:
"We successfully launched the CRM system two weeks ahead of schedule
with 95% user adoption within the first month, compared to 40% in
previous attempts. The project was recognized by senior leadership,
and I was promoted to Senior Project Manager. More importantly, I
learned the value of stakeholder engagement and iterative progress."
```

### Example 2: Problem-Solving

**Question**: "Describe a time when you solved a complex problem."

**STAR Answer**:

```
Situation:
"At my current company, we noticed our customer churn rate had
increased from 5% to 12% over six months, which was costing us
approximately $2 million annually."

Task:
"I was asked to identify the root cause and develop a solution to
reduce churn back to acceptable levels within the quarter."

Action:
"I conducted a data analysis of churned customers and found that 70%
left within 90 days of purchase. I then interviewed 50 churned
customers to understand why. The data revealed that customers felt
overwhelmed during onboarding. I proposed and implemented a three-part
solution: First, I redesigned our onboarding process into bite-sized
modules. Second, I assigned dedicated success managers for the first
90 days. Third, I created automated check-ins at days 7, 30, and 60."

Result:
"Within three months, churn dropped to 6%, saving approximately
$1.2 million annually. Customer satisfaction scores during onboarding
improved from 7.2 to 8.9 out of 10. This experience taught me the
importance of data-driven decision making and listening to customer
feedback."
```

## 3️⃣ Common Interview Questions (25분)

### Category 1: Background & Experience

```
1. "Tell me about yourself."

Strong Answer Structure:
- Present (30 sec): Current role and key responsibilities
- Past (45 sec): Relevant experience and achievements
- Future (15 sec): Why you're interested in this role

Example:
"I'm currently a Senior Marketing Manager at TechCorp, where I lead
a team of eight and manage a $5M annual budget. Over the past three
years, I've launched 15 successful campaigns that generated $20M in
revenue.

Before TechCorp, I spent four years at StartupXYZ, where I grew the
marketing function from scratch, establishing our brand presence in
three new markets. My proudest achievement was increasing qualified
leads by 300% while reducing CAC by 40%.

I'm excited about this opportunity because your company's mission to
democratize education aligns with my personal values, and the scope
of this role would allow me to leverage my experience while tackling
new challenges in a fast-growing market."

---

2. "Why do you want to work here?"

Bad answer: "I need a job" / "The salary is good"

Good answer structure:
- Company mission/values alignment
- Specific aspects that excite you
- How you can contribute

Example:
"Three main reasons draw me to this company. First, your mission to
make healthcare accessible resonates deeply with me—my mother
struggled with healthcare access, so this is personal. Second, I'm
impressed by your innovative approach to telemedicine; the platform
you've built is truly groundbreaking. Third, this role offers the
perfect intersection of my technical skills and business acumen.
I believe my experience scaling SaaS products could help accelerate
your growth in the enterprise segment."

---

3. "Why are you leaving your current job?"

Bad answers:
✗ "My boss is terrible"
✗ "The company is failing"
✗ "I'm bored"

Good answer (stay positive!):
"I've had a great experience at my current company and learned a lot,
but I'm ready for my next challenge. Specifically, I'm looking for
three things: first, an opportunity to work on larger-scale projects
that impact more users; second, a chance to lead a bigger team and
develop my leadership skills; and third, a role where I can leverage
my technical background more strategically. This position offers all
three, which is why I'm so excited about it."

---

4. "What are your strengths?"

Structure:
- Name the strength
- Provide evidence
- Show relevance to the role

Example:
"One of my key strengths is analytical problem-solving. In my current
role, I'm known for diving deep into data to uncover insights that
others miss. For example, when our conversion rate dropped, instead
of making surface-level changes, I analyzed user behavior across
10,000 sessions and discovered that a single form field was causing
60% of drop-offs. That one change increased conversions by 35%.
This analytical approach would be valuable in this role, especially
given your focus on data-driven growth."
```

### Category 2: Behavioral Questions

```
5. "Tell me about a time you failed."

STAR Framework:
Situation: "In my first year as Product Manager, I launched a feature
that I was convinced would increase engagement."

Task: "I was responsible for the entire product roadmap and this was
a flagship feature."

Action: "I relied heavily on my intuition and limited user research.
I pushed the team to build it quickly, ignoring some concerns from
engineers about technical debt."

Result: "The feature launched, but user adoption was only 8% versus
our 40% goal. Worse, it introduced bugs that affected core
functionality. I had to make the difficult decision to roll it back.
However, this failure taught me invaluable lessons. Now, I never ship
without robust user research, I listen more carefully to my engineering
team, and I build MVPs to test assumptions. In fact, using this new
approach, my next three feature launches all exceeded targets by 20%+.
That failure made me a much better Product Manager."

Key: Own it + Show learning + Demonstrate growth

---

6. "Tell me about a conflict with a colleague."

Situation: "I was working on a cross-functional project with the
Sales team, and there was significant disagreement about our go-to-
market strategy."

Task: "I needed to align both teams around a unified approach before
our product launch in four weeks."

Action: "Rather than escalating to management, I requested a one-on-
one conversation with the Sales lead. I started by genuinely listening
to his concerns—he felt Marketing didn't understand customer pain
points. I acknowledged this and proposed we spend a day together on
sales calls. After that experience, I better understood his
perspective. We then co-created a hybrid strategy that incorporated
both our insights."

Result: "The launch exceeded our targets by 40%, and our cross-
functional relationship improved dramatically. We now have weekly
sync meetings, and that Sales lead is one of my strongest advocates.
This taught me that most conflicts stem from lack of understanding,
and investing time in listening pays huge dividends."

---

7. "Describe a time you showed leadership."

Situation: "During a critical product launch, our Engineering Lead
unexpectedly left the company two weeks before the deadline."

Task: "As Product Manager, I needed to keep the team motivated and
ensure we still hit our deadline."

Action: "I called an emergency team meeting and was transparent about
the situation. I asked for everyone's input on how to move forward.
Together, we redistributed the work, and I personally took on more
technical coordination tasks. I also scheduled daily 15-minute standups
to maintain alignment and provide support. Throughout, I maintained
an optimistic, solution-focused tone."

Result: "We launched on time with zero critical bugs. The team's
morale actually improved because everyone felt heard and empowered.
Three team members specifically mentioned this experience in their
year-end reviews as a highlight. It taught me that leadership isn't
about having all the answers—it's about bringing people together and
enabling them to succeed."
```

### Category 3: Situational Questions

```
8. "How would you handle an angry customer?"

"I'd follow a four-step approach. First, I'd listen actively without
interrupting, showing empathy—sometimes people just need to be heard.
Second, I'd acknowledge their frustration and apologize for the
inconvenience, even if it wasn't directly our fault. Third, I'd focus
on solutions, asking what would make the situation right and finding
a path forward. Fourth, I'd follow up personally to ensure the issue
was resolved. In my previous role, I used this approach with a
customer threatening to cancel a $200K contract. By really listening,
I discovered the root issue was a training gap, not product quality.
We provided personalized training, and they're now our biggest
advocate."

---

9. "What would you do if you disagreed with your manager?"

"I believe in respectful disagreement. I'd first ensure I fully
understand their perspective—sometimes disagreements stem from
incomplete information. Then, I'd request a one-on-one conversation
and present my viewpoint with data and specific examples. I'd frame
it as 'Here's what I'm seeing that makes me concerned,' rather than
'You're wrong.' Ultimately, if we still disagree, I'd support their
decision while perhaps asking if we could revisit it after gathering
more data. In my experience, managers appreciate thoughtful pushback
as long as it's constructive and data-driven."

---

10. "How do you prioritize when everything is urgent?"

"I use a framework based on impact and urgency. First, I identify
which tasks truly drive business results versus which just feel
urgent. Second, I consider dependencies—what's blocking others?
Third, I communicate transparently with stakeholders about trade-offs.
For example, last quarter I had three 'urgent' requests. I created
a one-pager showing the expected outcome and resource requirements
for each, then facilitated a discussion with stakeholders to align on
priorities. This approach transformed a stressful situation into a
collaborative decision-making process."
```

## 4️⃣ Salary Negotiation (15분)

### Negotiation Framework

```
Research Phase:
✓ Know market rates (Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, Blind)
✓ Know your worth (experience, skills, results)
✓ Know your walk-away number

Question: "What are your salary expectations?"

Bad answers:
✗ "I'll take whatever you offer"
✗ "I need at least $X"
✗ "I don't know"

Good answer (deflect initially):
"I'm more focused on finding the right fit and opportunity for growth.
Could you share the range you've budgeted for this role?"

OR (if you must give a number):
"Based on my research of the market, my experience, and the value I'd
bring to this role, I'm looking for something in the range of
$X to $Y. However, I'm flexible depending on the overall compensation
package and growth opportunities."

---

When you receive an offer:

1. Express enthusiasm:
"Thank you so much for the offer. I'm very excited about the
opportunity to join the team."

2. Request time:
"Would it be possible to have a few days to review the details?"

3. Research and prepare:
- Compare to market rates
- Calculate total compensation
- Identify gaps

4. Negotiate (if appropriate):

Example script:
"Thank you again for the offer. I'm genuinely excited about joining
[Company] and contributing to [specific goal]. I've given this careful
thought, and based on market rates for this role and my experience,
I was hoping we could discuss the base salary.

I'm seeing similar roles in [market/industry] typically ranging from
$X to $Y. Given my [specific skills/experience], would it be possible
to start at $Z?

I'm confident I can deliver significant value, particularly in
[specific area], and I'm committed to growing with the company
long-term."

5. Be prepared to justify:
- Specific achievements
- Unique skills
- Market data
- Other offers (if true)

6. Consider the whole package:
- Base salary
- Bonus/commission
- Equity
- Benefits
- Vacation
- Remote work
- Professional development budget
- Title

7. Get it in writing:
"Thank you for working with me on this. Could you send over the
updated offer in writing?"
```

## 5️⃣ Questions to Ask the Interviewer (10분)

### About the Role

```
✓ "What does success look like in this role after 6 months? After
   a year?"

✓ "What are the biggest challenges facing the person in this role?"

✓ "Can you describe a typical day or week in this position?"

✓ "What are the key priorities for this role in the first 90 days?"

✓ "How would you describe the team dynamics?"

✓ "What's the growth path for this role?"
```

### About the Team

```
✓ "Can you tell me about the team I'd be working with?"

✓ "How does this team collaborate with other departments?"

✓ "What's your management style?"

✓ "How does the team handle disagreements or conflicts?"

✓ "What do you enjoy most about working here?"
```

### About the Company

```
✓ "What are the company's top priorities this year?"

✓ "How has the company changed in the past year?"

✓ "What do you see as the company's competitive advantage?"

✓ "Can you describe the company culture?"

✓ "What are the biggest opportunities and challenges ahead?"
```

### Strategic Questions

```
✓ "If you could change one thing about working here, what would it be?"

✓ "What would you say sets apart your top performers?"

✓ "How does the company support professional development?"

✓ "What's the next step in the interview process?"
```

## 📌 Interview Success Tips

```
Before the interview:
✓ Research the company thoroughly
✓ Prepare 5-7 STAR stories
✓ Practice out loud
✓ Prepare questions to ask
✓ Test your tech (for video)
✓ Choose professional attire
✓ Arrive/log in 10 minutes early

During the interview:
✓ Smile and make eye contact
✓ Listen carefully before answering
✓ Use the STAR method
✓ Be specific with examples
✓ Ask clarifying questions if needed
✓ Show enthusiasm
✓ Take notes

After the interview:
✓ Send thank-you email within 24 hours
✓ Reflect on what went well/improve
✓ Follow up if you don't hear back
✓ Continue job search until you sign
```

**💡 Remember**:
Interviews are two-way conversations. You're evaluating them as much
as they're evaluating you. Be yourself, be prepared, and be confident
in the value you bring!
