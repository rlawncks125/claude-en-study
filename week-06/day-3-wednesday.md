## 1️⃣ Negotiation Fundamentals (20분)

### BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement)
```
정의: 협상이 결렬될 경우의 최선의 대안

Why Important:
- 협상력의 기준점
- Walk-away point 결정
- 자신감의 원천

Example:
Job Offer Negotiation
- Your BATNA: Current job ($90K) + offer from Company B ($95K)
- Their offer: $92K
- Decision: Decline and counter, because your BATNA is better

Strengthening Your BATNA:
✓ Develop multiple options
✓ Improve your current situation
✓ Create competition
✓ Demonstrate alternatives (subtly)

Language:
"While I'm very interested in this role, I'm also exploring other
opportunities that might be a better fit."
```

### ZOPA (Zone of Possible Agreement)
```
정의: 양측이 합의할 수 있는 범위

Example: Salary Negotiation
Candidate's range: $100K (minimum) - $120K (target)
Company's range: $95K (minimum) - $110K (maximum)

ZOPA: $100K - $110K (overlap)

If no overlap = No deal possible (unless one side moves)

Strategy:
- Research to understand their range
- Never reveal your bottom line
- Start outside ZOPA to leave room
```

## 2️⃣ Negotiation Frameworks (25분)

### Harvard Negotiation Framework
```
1. Interests, not positions
Bad: "I want $120K" (position)
Good: "I need compensation that reflects my 10 years of experience
and allows me to support my family" (interest)

2. Separate people from the problem
"I understand you have budget constraints. Let's explore creative
solutions that work for both of us."

3. Generate options for mutual gain
"What if we start at $X base salary but include performance bonuses
tied to metrics we both care about?"

4. Use objective criteria
"Based on Glassdoor data for this role in this market with my
experience level, the median is $Y."
```

### Persuasion Techniques

#### Reciprocity
```
Principle: People feel obligated to return favors

Application:
"I'm willing to be flexible on start date to accommodate your needs.
In return, I'm hoping we can revisit the salary to better reflect
market rates."

Language:
"I'll give you X if you can help me with Y"
```

#### Scarcity
```
Principle: People value what's scarce

Application (use ethically!):
"I'm very interested in this role, but I do need to make a decision
by Friday as I have another offer pending."

Language:
"This timeline works, but I want to be transparent that..."
```

#### Authority
```
Principle: People defer to experts

Application:
"In my 15 years in this industry, I've found that..."
"Industry research from McKinsey shows..."

Language:
"Based on extensive research..." / "Experts agree that..."
```

#### Consistency
```
Principle: People want to act consistently with commitments

Application:
"Earlier you mentioned quality is your top priority. This premium
option aligns with that value."

Language:
"You said... so it makes sense to..."
```

#### Liking
```
Principle: We say yes to people we like

Application:
- Build rapport first
- Find common ground
- Compliment sincerely

Language:
"I really appreciate your transparency" / "We both want..."
```

#### Social Proof
```
Principle: People follow what others do

Application:
"70% of companies in this space have adopted this approach"
"Your competitors are already using..."

Language:
"Leading companies like..." / "Industry standard is..."
```

## 3️⃣ Negotiation Scenarios (25분)

### Scenario 1: Salary Negotiation
```
Situation: You received an offer of $95K. You want $110K.

Research:
- Market rate: $100-$120K
- Your current: $90K
- Your BATNA: Another offer at $100K

Strategy:
1. Express enthusiasm
2. Anchor high
3. Justify with data
4. Be flexible on other terms
5. Get it in writing

Conversation:
You: "Thank you for the offer. I'm excited about this opportunity.
I've given this careful thought, and based on my research of market
rates for this role, combined with my 8 years of relevant experience,
I was hoping we could discuss the base salary.

I'm seeing similar positions ranging from $100K to $120K. Given my
track record of [specific achievements], would it be possible to
start at $110K?

I'm flexible on other aspects of the package, and I'm confident I'll
deliver significant value, particularly in [specific area]."

If they say no:
You: "I understand there may be budget constraints. Are there other
components we could adjust? For example:
- Sign-on bonus
- Performance bonus structure
- Equity
- Additional vacation days
- Professional development budget
- Earlier performance review

What flexibility do you have in these areas?"

If still no acceptable offer:
You: "I really appreciate you working with me on this. I need a day
to think it over. Can I get back to you tomorrow?"

[Then evaluate against your BATNA]
```

### Scenario 2: Vendor Contract
```
Situation: Negotiating with a software vendor

Their initial offer: $100K/year, 3-year contract, 5% annual increase
Your target: $75K/year, 1-year trial, flat rate

Opening:
You: "Thank you for the proposal. We're definitely interested in
your solution. I'd like to discuss a few points to ensure this
works for both of us.

First, given we're new to this type of solution and need to prove ROI
internally, would you be open to a one-year pilot program instead of
a three-year commitment?"

[Wait for response]

Pricing:
You: "On pricing, $100K is above our allocated budget. We've budgeted
$75K for this initiative. Additionally, we're evaluating two other
vendors with similar capabilities at $80K and $85K.

What flexibility do you have on pricing, especially if we can commit
to a longer evaluation timeline or agree to be a case study?"

Value-Add Negotiation:
You: "If $75K isn't feasible, what additional value could we get at
$100K? For example:
- Additional user licenses
- Premium support tier
- Customization services
- Training for our team
- Integration with our existing systems

What can you do to make this a win-win?"

Competitive Pressure (use carefully):
You: "We're big believers in your product, but I need to build a
business case for my CFO. Your competitor offered $80K with similar
features. Help me understand what justifies the $20K premium."

Closing:
You: "Here's what would work for us: $85K for year one with option
to extend at the same rate if we're satisfied. Can you make that
happen?"
```

### Scenario 3: Difficult Stakeholder
```
Situation: Stakeholder blocking your project

Stakeholder: "I don't think this project is a priority. We have more
urgent issues to address."

You (acknowledge + probe):
"I appreciate your perspective, and I agree we have competing
priorities. Can you help me understand what concerns you most about
this project? Is it the resource allocation, timeline, or something
else?"

[Listen carefully]

You (reframe):
"I hear you on [their concern]. What if we could structure this to
address your priority while still moving forward? For example, what
if we:
- Start with a smaller pilot
- Use existing resources without new hires
- Show results in 30 days instead of 90
- Align metrics with your department's OKRs

Would any of these address your concerns?"

You (create allies):
"Sarah from Finance and Tom from Operations both see value in this
approach. Could we set up a 30-minute meeting where they share their
perspective? I think you'd find it valuable."

You (escalation if needed):
"I respect your position. If we can't find common ground, would it
make sense to bring this to [mutual manager] to get their input on
prioritization?"
```

## 4️⃣ Advanced Tactics (15분)

### Silence
```
Power of silence after making an offer:
- Let them talk first
- Don't fill uncomfortable pauses
- Wait for them to counter

Example:
You: "Based on everything we've discussed, I believe $X is fair."
[Then STOP talking. Wait.]
```

### Anchoring
```
Go first with an ambitious but reasonable anchor:

High Anchor (seller):
"Our rate for this type of project is $200K."
[Sets high reference point]

Low Anchor (buyer):
"We've budgeted $50K for this initiative."
[Sets low reference point]
```

### The Flinch
```
React to their offer:
"Ooh, that's much higher than I expected."
[Often triggers immediate concession]
```

### Good Cop / Bad Cop
```
"I'd love to approve this, but my CFO will never agree to that price.
Can you help me make a case to him with a better number?"
```

### Time Pressure
```
Create (or use existing) deadlines:
"I need to finalize this by Friday for budget approval."
"Our fiscal year ends next month."
```

## 5️⃣ Daily Assessment (100점)

### Part A: BATNA & ZOPA Analysis (20점)
**Scenario**: You're negotiating a consulting contract.
- Your costs: $50K
- Your target: $100K
- Client's budget: $60-$90K
- Your BATNA: Another client at $75K

**Tasks**:
1. Identify ZOPA
2. What's your walk-away point?
3. What's your opening offer and why?

### Part B: Negotiation Strategy (40점)
**Scenario**: Write a negotiation strategy (400-500 words)

You're buying software for your company:
- Vendor's price: $150K/year
- Your budget: $100K/year
- Alternatives: Competitor at $120K (less features)
- Your leverage: 500 user licenses, 3-year potential

Include:
- Your BATNA
- Opening position
- Negotiation tactics
- Concession strategy

### Part C: Role Play Script (40점)
**Task**: Write both sides of a negotiation (600-800 words)

Scenario: Salary negotiation
- Offer: $100K
- You want: $120K
- Market rate: $110-$130K

Write 10-15 exchanges showing:
- Anchoring
- Justification
- Handling objections
- Finding creative solutions
- Closing
