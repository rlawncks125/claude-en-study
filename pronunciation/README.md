## 🔊 Notation Guide | 표기법 가이드

### IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet)
Standard phonetic notation used worldwide. Each symbol represents one sound.
- Example: business /ˈbɪznəs/

### Korean Approximation | 한글 근사음
Korean characters to help approximate English sounds (note: these are approximations only)
- Example: business ≈ 비즈니스 (but with proper English sounds)

### Stress Markers | 강세 표시
- **PRIMARY STRESS:** /ˈ/ before the stressed syllable (주 강세)
- **Secondary stress:** /ˌ/ before the syllable (부 강세)
- Example: communication /kəˌmjuːnɪˈkeɪʃn/

### Mouth Position Icons | 입 모양 아이콘
- 👄 Lips (입술 모양)
- 👅 Tongue (혀 위치)
- 🗣️ Voice (유성음/무성음)

## 💡 Key Tips for Korean Speakers | 한국인 학습자를 위한 핵심 팁

### 1. Common Challenges | 주요 어려움
- Korean has fewer vowel distinctions than English (한국어는 영어보다 모음 구분이 적음)
- Korean doesn't have certain consonants: f, v, z, θ, ð
- Korean syllable structure doesn't allow complex consonant clusters (복잡한 자음군 불가)
- Korean has less prominent stress patterns (한국어는 강세가 약함)
- Korean speakers often add vowels between consonants (자음 사이에 모음 추가 경향)

### 2. Success Strategies | 성공 전략
- **Record yourself (녹음하기):** Compare with native speakers
- **Exaggerate at first (과장하기):** Make sounds bigger than needed, then reduce
- **Focus on mouth position (입 모양 집중):** Physical positioning is key
- **Practice minimal pairs (최소 대립쌍 연습):** Train your ear and mouth simultaneously
- **Slow down (천천히):** Accuracy first, speed later
- **Daily practice (매일 연습):** 20-30 minutes is better than 2 hours once a week
- **Be patient (인내심):** Some sounds may take months to master

### 3. Priority Sounds for Business English | 우선순위 발음

Focus on these high-impact areas first:
1. **TH sounds (θ, ð)** - Very common, highly noticeable
2. **R and L distinction** - Changes word meanings
3. **F and V** - Essential for professional credibility
4. **Final consonants** - Important for clarity
5. **Word stress** - Critical for understanding
6. **Sentence intonation** - Conveys meaning and emotion
